# Cards Against Humanity — Rules

*A party game for horrible people. This fixture ships a ~55-card taste
of the real ~500 White / ~90 Black card Base Set — enough to play a
short game and to exercise the tabletop-format platform's `"cah"`
card_style renderer end to end. See README.md for scope and licensing.*

## Contents (this fixture)

- **40 White Cards** — answers: people, places, things, and depravities.
- **15 Black Cards** — prompts: fill-in-the-blank statements and
  questions. Most ask for **1** white card; two are marked **PICK 2**,
  asking for two.

## Players

3–20. Best with 4 to 10. Whoever isn't the Card Czar for a round
submits an answer that round; the Czar doesn't.

## Setup

1. Shuffle the White deck and deal **10 White Cards** to each player.
2. Shuffle the Black deck and set it face down in the middle of the
   table.
3. Pick a first Card Czar however your table likes (worst haircut goes
   first is traditional).

## Playing a round

1. The Card Czar draws the top Black Card and reads it aloud — prompt
   or fill-in-the-blank.
2. Every other player privately chooses the funniest White Card(s) in
   their hand as their answer and passes them face down to the Czar.
   - Normally that's **one** card.
   - If the Black Card says **PICK 2** (see `attributes.pick`), each
     player submits *two* White Cards, in the order they want them
     read — order matters, since the Czar reads both cards together as
     one combined answer.
3. The Czar shuffles all the submissions face down, then reads each
   combination aloud against the Black Card.
4. The Czar — and only the Czar — picks the funniest answer. Whoever
   submitted it wins the round and keeps the Black Card as one Awesome
   Point.
5. Everyone draws back up to 10 White Cards. The player to the Czar's
   left becomes the new Czar, and a new round begins.

## Winning

There's no fixed end. The traditional target is Awesome Points equal to
the number of players at the table, but most groups just play until the
snacks or the will to live run out, whichever comes first.

> Because the Czar never submits an answer in their own round, everyone
> gets an equal number of judged rounds only if the player count evenly
> divides the number of rounds played — a house rule to track if your table
> cares about exact fairness.

## House rules worth knowing

- **Rando Cardrissian**: with a small group, deal a hand to an imaginary
  extra player and flip their top card blind into every round as a free
  entry — it wins more often than it has any right to.
- **Rotating Czar only**: the Czar never submits an answer in their own
  round, so nobody judges their own joke.
- Trading/packet-swap variants and other browser-forum house rules exist
  but aren't canonical — this fixture only encodes the base rules above.
